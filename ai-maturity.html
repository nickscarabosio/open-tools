AI Organizational Maturity

Not a tech ladder.
How much of the company has reorganized around AI — from one person’s openness to a redesigned org.

human capability → productive capacity
L0
Openness
Am I open to the possibility that AI changes how I work?

Disposition, not deployment. Closed here blocks everything above.

Closed signals: avoidance & delay, “not for my role,” one bad attempt then quit, quiet refusal to change how work gets done.

L1
Individual Productivity
How can AI help me do my work faster?

Same work, faster. Trap: adoption ≠ institutionalization.

Tools: Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot. Drafting, summarizing, docs, code. Typical gain: 20–30% personal productivity.

Institutionalized L1 has standardized prompts, expectations, governance, and measured workflows.

L2
Workflow Redesign
How should this workflow change because AI exists?

Rebuild the process before agents. Most orgs skip this.

Hiring, onboarding, sales, finance, support — rebuilt around AI before agents deploy.

Failure mode: insert AI into the same process. Same shape = same speed.

L3
AI Operating Teams
What work should AI permanently own?

Persistent AI teammates, not one-off agents. Work runs continuous.

10–50 teammates by role complexity. Sales: pipeline, proposal, CRM, meeting. Finance: AP, forecast, cash, contract. Marketing: research, content, analytics.

L4
AI-Native Organization
If AI existed from day one, how would we build this company differently?

Redesign the company. AI executes; humans judge.

Org chart and decision rights redesigned. Wider spans. Fewer layers. Teams around outcomes, not departments.

Startups will default here. Enterprise incumbents will lag.

The trap

L1 → L3. Skip L2.

Vendors sell “AI employees” into unbroken processes.

L2 is what makes L3 pay.
